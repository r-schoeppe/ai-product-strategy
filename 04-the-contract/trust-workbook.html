Why trust is the product here

The stakes are a game

A coach changes their defence because Scout says "93% quick slant". A wrong tell costs a game. Like the Air Canada chatbot, the report speaks for the brand.

Anyone can write a summary

Google, Hudl and WatchGameFilm can add a summary quickly. What they cannot copy quickly is evidence on every claim, a published test gate, and a corrections history built from your coaches.

It costs almost nothing

The numbers are computed by ordinary code and the model only writes them up. Reliability comes from the design, not from an expensive model, so it fits the 7% AI cost share from Module 3.

The core design in one lineConfidence here is evidence, not the model's opinion of itself. "13 of 14 plays across 5 games" can be checked by the coach; "the AI is 92% sure" cannot.

The reliability contract

What Scout promises, how it is measured, and what happens when it breaks. Targets sit inside the defensible bands from the slides, not the vanity numbers.

Why the fallback matters

Graceful degradationThe prototype already shows the plain computed report. If the model is wrong, slow or down, the coach still gets a correct, if less polished, report. Most AI products have no such floor, and ours is free.

Human in the loop: a shrinking queue, not babysitting

1 Auto checks

Every report: number fidelity, claim-to-stat mapping, injection scan.

2 Flag

Low tier, a coach "Wrong" verdict, a failed check or an injection flag.

3 Review

The owner today, a rotating reviewer later. A coach panel for the monthly audit.

4 Feed back

Each confirmed error becomes a golden row; tag fixes improve the consistency checks.

Target shape of the review queue (a target, not data)

Why it worksAt launch every "Wrong" flag is reviewed. Once the gate is stable only a sample is, and the share of flagged reports falls as the golden set grows. Without the feedback step it would be a permanent crutch; with it, each correction pays for itself.

Red team: failures I missed, played by your partner (Claude)

1 Biased sample

If the league's cut files contain only some plays, such as highlights, every tendency is skewed, and the numbers still look precise. Fix: a film completeness check per game (expected plays against tagged), shown as a data-quality note; open question to confirm with the league.

2 Instructions hidden in file names

WebDAV file names and play notes are written by other people. Fix: treated as data only; scanned and flagged; golden rows 17 and 18.

3 Stale tendencies

An opponent changes in mid-season and the report says "they always". Fix: recent games weigh more, the report shows the date of the latest film and flags changes; golden row 8.

Other gaps still open

  • Garbage in: validators check the wording against the data, not the data itself. Tag-consistency checks and a data-quality score reduce this (rows 9 and 10).
  • Over-trust in the High label: coaches may read it as a prediction. Counts are shown next to percentages and the wording says "tendency", not "will" (row 19).
  • Cross-team leakage in the chat (row 21), and youth player video privacy, which moves to Module 5.

The pitch, before and after Module 4

M1 baseline

Flag football coaches will prepare for opponents with a TapeMine AI scouting summary built from tagged play video, because league-supplied clips now make that scouting data available without manual film work.

Now

TapeMine Scout is an evidence-backed scouting report for amateur 5-on-5 flag football: every tendency links to the plays behind it, shows how much evidence supports it, and ships only if it passes a published test gate. At about €10 per team with the AI at only 7% of cost, we compete on flag-specific depth, price, usability and trust, not on model quality. The risks we manage are video cost (93% of COGS), whether coaches accept the higher base price, and the thin data of amateur teams.
